# BizBridge Lean Business Canvas

## Problem

- Young founders and township or rural businesses may have fragmented sales,
  expense, and payment records.
- Owners can struggle to understand cash flow or prepare a clear business
  profile for funding and formal-market conversations.
- Finding relevant procurement opportunities and judging payment concerns can
  be difficult.

## Customer segments

- Young entrepreneurs and micro/small-business owners in township and rural
  communities.
- Initial examples for testing: informal caterers, local service providers, and
  small retailers.
- Potential partners to validate later: entrepreneurship hubs, business
  support organisations, and procurement programmes.

## Unique value proposition

**A practical bridge from informal business activity to organised, explainable
records and next steps.**

BizBridge combines a business profile, transaction tracking, cash-flow views,
readiness checklist, payment-reference review alerts, and opportunity discovery
in one lightweight workflow.

## Solution

- Capture a business profile and income/expense transactions.
- Show recorded cash flow and spending patterns with clear limits.
- Build a readiness passport from business records and explicit self-reported
  preparation steps.
- Surface procurement opportunities and payment references that need review.

## Channels

- Local entrepreneurship hubs, community organisations, and hackathon
  demonstrations.
- Founder referrals and practical business workshops.
- Partnerships with organisations supporting small businesses, subject to
  validation.

## Revenue streams

- To validate: a free owner-facing core product with optional paid support or
  partner-funded services.
- To validate: organisational subscriptions for cohorts or business-support
  programmes.
- No pricing or willingness-to-pay evidence has been collected yet.

## Cost structure

- Product development, hosting, backups, monitoring, and security maintenance.
- User onboarding and business-support content.
- Partner integration and opportunity-data maintenance if those capabilities
  are expanded.

## Key metrics

- Businesses completing a profile and adding a first transaction.
- Returning businesses recording transactions over multiple weeks.
- Readiness checklist completion and repeat visits to next steps.
- Opportunity views and applications, where application workflows are enabled.
- User-reported usefulness and data-correction rates.

## Unfair advantage

- A workflow focused on a small business's journey from starting and recording
  activity to preparing for more formal market access.
- Trust-oriented explanations that distinguish recorded data, self-report, and
  unverified alerts.
- This is a hypothesis to test, not a proven moat.

## Riskiest assumptions to validate

1. Owners are willing and able to record transactions consistently.
2. The checklist maps to real next steps requested by local support
   organisations and buyers.
3. Opportunity data can be kept relevant, legitimate, and up to date.
4. Users understand the limits of self-reported readiness and historical
   financial insights.

## First validation experiments

- Observe five target founders entering profile and transaction information.
- Ask two business-support practitioners to review the checklist wording.
- Test whether a founder can explain their own cash-flow trend after a short
  walkthrough.
- Measure whether opportunity listings lead to a useful next action.
